# Sound: importing it, and checking it without ears

Verified on Unreal Engine 5.9.0 (changelist 60351404, the launcher's install) on macOS 26.6, Apple Silicon (M4 Max), from 10 to 17 November 2026, while building two small games: a sports game, then a runner made for a game jam. Where a line comes from reading the engine source rather than from a run, it says so. Names starting `game.` or `GAME_` stand in for that game's console variables and actor labels: choose your own.

All of this ran in the game's dressed level on 5.9.0 (21 sounds: one-shots, an ambience loop, two music loops).

- **Import with the shipped tool.** `EditorToolset.SoundWaveToolset.ImportFile` takes `folderPath`, `assetName`, `sourceFile`, `bReplaceExisting`, `bSave` (the last two default to false) and declares wav, aif, aiff, ogg, flac, opus and mp3. 16-bit FLAC came in as 48 kHz Sound Waves with the source's length and channel count. `ExportWav` (`soundWave={"refPath": ...}`, `filePath`) writes one back out: four exports differed from their sources by at most 2 steps of 16-bit audio, so FLAC sources are safe and a third of the size of WAV in the repository.
- **Reading a Sound Wave in Python.** There is no `get_duration()`. `get_editor_property` works for `duration`, `num_channels`, `sample_rate`, `total_samples` and `looping`; `set_editor_property("looping", True)` then a forced save makes a loop.
- **Record what the game outputs.** The class is `unreal.AudioMixerLibrary` in Python (not `AudioMixerBlueprintLibrary`). During PIE: `start_recording_output(world, 40.0)`, let the game run, then `stop_recording_output(world, unreal.AudioRecordingExportType.WAV_FILE, "name", "folder")` writes `<Project>/Saved/BouncedWavFiles/folder/name.wav` (48 kHz stereo). It will not write anywhere else.
- **The editor mutes an unfocused game.** `BaseEngine.ini` sets `[Audio] UnfocusedVolumeMultiplier=0.0`, and `AudioDevice.cpp` multiplies the primary volume by it, so a PIE session driven over MCP while a terminal has focus is silent on the speakers. The probe set `au.DisableAppVolume 1` for the length of the recording and the recording had sound (peak -9.8 dBFS); recording without it was not tried. With it set, the game is also audible on the speakers, so say so before running it.
- **Game time and the recording drift.** The recording runs on real time; the event log on game time. Over 32 s the recording fell 0.68 s behind the log, 0.5 s of it in one editor hitch. Match each logged event to the nearest burst of sound and carry the offset forward; do not assume it is zero. Done that way, 19 of 19 logged swings, hits, serves and bounces had a burst at their moment.
- **Make the sound a probe.** The game's audio object counts every cue it is asked for in a `TMap<FName, int32>` debug property; Python reads it with `dict(audio.get_editor_property("plays").items())`. Count the request even when no sound is found, so "the game asked" and "it was heard" are separate checks.
- **C++ that compiled and played:** find a sound by name with `FPackageName::DoesPackageExist` before `LoadObject<USoundBase>` (a missing sound is then silence with no log warning); one-shots with `UGameplayStatics::PlaySound2D(WorldContext, Sound, Volume, Pitch)`; loops on components from `UGameplayStatics::CreateSound2D(WorldContext, Sound, 1, 1, 0, nullptr, false, false)` (last argument: do not auto-destroy), held in `UPROPERTY`s, with `SetVolumeMultiplier` each tick for cross-fades and ducking. Use a separate `FRandomStream` for pitch and variant choice: drawing from the game's seeded stream changes the rally.
- **Load every sound up front, or its first play is late.** `UGameplayStatics::PrimeSound(Sound)` on each sound when the level begins. A second game on 5.9.0 (84 Sound Waves, 12 November 2026) measured the gap between asking for a sharp sound and its burst in a recording: without it, the first play of each of three landing sounds came 0.13 to 0.16 s after it was asked for and later plays 0.04 s; with it, the median over ten sharp cues was 0.035 s and the longest 0.11 s.
- **Pause a loop that must keep its place; do not fade it to nothing.** `UAudioComponent::SetPaused(true)` once the volume reaches zero, `SetPaused(false)` before raising it. Checked by correlating a recording against the music file: a loop paused 28 s in was 30.0 s in two seconds after it was resumed, where a restart would have been at 2 s. Whether a loop left playing at zero volume is dropped and restarted was not tried.
  - A reel that winds down and spins back up is `SetPitchMultiplier` ramped to about 0.35 over a quarter of a second while the volume falls, then the pause; and the reverse on the way back.
- **Log cues on real time.** The recording runs on real time, so a cue log stamped with `GetWorld()->GetRealTimeSeconds()` needs no drift tracking: over 93 s the recording's length and the log's span agreed to 0.1 s.
- **Do not find the recording's offset from a sound that repeats.** Footsteps came every 0.2 s, and a best-fit offset over all sharp cues settled on 0.24 s, one footstep late, when the true delay was 0.035 s. Measure the delay from sharp cues that stand alone (a landing after a jump, a clapperboard), then judge the repeating ones with it.
- **An editor hitch can swallow a sound's moment.** Two of 96 footsteps had silence where they were logged; each sat on a frame of about 0.4 s, where the log had a gap too. Do not chase those in the mix.
- **A loop flag set by a name prefix will catch the wrong files.** An import script that looped everything named `BGM_` also looped two one-shot stings. Carry "loops" in the list the import reads.
- **Trap:** `AudioMixerLibrary.get_current_audio_output_device_name(world)` raises: it needs a delegate argument.

## The mix, measured

Run on 5.9.0 on a Mac, 12 November 2026, in the second game (84 Sound Waves, then 94), with the levels read from recordings made as above.

- **Build each kind of sound to a target, then set one output gain from a recording of play.** One-shots: the loudest single event kept, silence cut, mono, peak at -1 dBFS. Ambience loops: -26 LUFS. Music loops: -16 LUFS. At an output gain of 1, a take with music ran at -23 LUFS with its loudest moment at -6 dBFS; a gain of 1.5 (about 3.5 dB) on everything gave -20 to -17 LUFS, a loudest moment of -2 dBFS and no clipped samples. The title screen sat at about -23 LUFS and effects alone at -23. The music bus was 0.55.
- **To put a sound on one side of the picture, make the file mono and play it at a point beside the camera.** `UGameplayStatics::PlaySoundAtLocation` at the camera's position + 3 m forward + up to 3 m to the side, through a transient `USoundAttenuation` with `bAttenuate = false` and `bSpatialize = true`, so it is placed but as loud as anywhere. A stereo file stays in the middle. Check it from the recording, channel against channel: all 11 placed cues were louder on their side, a click by 7 to 8 dB and a burst by 2 to 4 dB.
- **Drive sound from the game's public state, compared with the last frame, not from input.** The sound actor read only the game mode's state; a replay then played its take's own sounds, end mark included (a probe of the endings passed 17 of 17), and another session changed the game's flow twice without touching the sound code.
- **Tie footsteps to distance at the run clip's stride, not to time.** Two steps in 0.4 s at 6.5 m/s is a footfall every 1.3 m, and the steps then stay in time with the feet at any speed, the testing key's fast-forward included.
- **Sound starts a median 31 ms after the game asks for it** (longest 80 ms) once every sound is primed; the first play of an unprimed sound was about 0.1 s later than that.
- **Generated sound has limits the engine has to cover.** A sound-effect generator made no usable wind-down (4 of 4 "film stopping" takes were a projector at a steady level: do it with pitch, above) and no sustained rise (5 of 6 "rising hiss" takes died in under half a second). Pick takes by measured envelope; the generation side is in the `sound-effects` and `music` skills.

Laying the game's own sound files under stepped footage: `capture-and-recording.md`, section 5.
